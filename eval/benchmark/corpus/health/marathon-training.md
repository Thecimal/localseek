# Marathon training plan

## Goal
Finish the autumn marathon in under four hours. That means an average pace of about 5:40 per kilometre, which is comfortable if the aerobic base is built carefully over twenty weeks.

## Weekly structure
Four runs per week: one easy run, one speed session, one medium-long run and one long run on the weekend. Two rest or cross-training days. Weekly distance grows from 35 to 65 kilometres, with every fourth week reduced by about a quarter to let the body absorb the work.

## Long runs
The long run grows by two kilometres every week and peaks at 32 kilometres three weeks before the race. Run the first two thirds at easy pace and the last third at goal pace. Never increase the long run and the weekly total in the same week.

## Speed work
Alternate between intervals such as six times 800 metres at 10K pace with 400 metres jogging recovery, and tempo runs of 20 to 40 minutes at a steady hard effort. Speed work is dropped entirely in recovery weeks.

## Strength and mobility
Two short sessions per week: single-leg squats, calf raises, hip bridges and planks. Fifteen minutes of mobility work after every run keeps the hips and ankles loose.

## Recovery
Sleep at least eight hours during peak weeks. Eat within an hour of finishing every long run. Take an easy day after every hard day, and skip a session rather than run through sharp pain.

## Nutrition in training
Eat a carbohydrate-rich dinner before long runs and a mixed breakfast on the morning itself. During runs longer than 90 minutes practise drinking little and often, so the stomach learns to cope with fluids at pace. Protein with every meal supports recovery, and a small snack within an hour of finishing helps refill the glycogen stores.

## Shoes and gear
Rotate two pairs of shoes so each has a day to recover, and retire them at about 700 kilometres. Wear the exact kit planned for race day on at least two long runs, including socks, shorts and any belt, to find out where chafing might appear before it matters.

## Common mistakes
Running easy days too fast, skipping recovery weeks, adding distance and intensity together, and ignoring early warning signs are the usual causes of a ruined build-up. Treat the plan as a guide and adjust it when life or illness gets in the way.

## Taper
Reduce the weekly distance to 75 percent three weeks before the race, 50 percent two weeks before and 30 percent in the final week. Keep a few short sections at race pace so the legs stay sharp, and avoid new shoes or new routines.

## Race day fuelling
Eat a familiar breakfast three hours before the start. During the race take one energy gel every 45 minutes starting at kilometre 10, with water at every aid station and an electrolyte drink every second stop. Do not try any new product on race day.
