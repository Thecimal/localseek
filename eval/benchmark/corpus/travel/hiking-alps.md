# Alps hiking trip

## Route overview
Two-day hut-to-hut traverse above the tree line, about 24 kilometres in total with 1,600 metres of climbing. The route follows a well-marked ridge path with two short sections protected by fixed cables. It is suitable for fit walkers with some mountain experience.

## Day one
Start from the valley car park at 07:30 and climb through larch forest to the first pass in about three hours. The path then crosses a boulder field before a long gentle traverse to the hut. Expect around six hours of walking including breaks, and carry lunch because there is no refreshment stop on the way.

## The hut
Book the hut well in advance because it fills up by early summer. Dinner and breakfast are included in the half-board price, and dormitory bunks come with blankets, so a light sleeping bag liner is enough. The hut does not accept cards, so bring cash.

## Day two
A steeper day with the summit scramble after the second pass, then a long descent on scree and meadow back to the valley. Allow seven to eight hours. The scramble is easy but exposed in places, and the descent is hard on tired legs, so poles are recommended.

## Gear
Sturdy boots, waterproof jacket, warm layer, hat and gloves, sun protection, two litres of water, headlamp, first-aid kit, map and a charged phone. Pack in dry bags in case of rain.

## Weather and timing
Mountain weather changes quickly, and afternoon thunderstorms are common in summer. Check the forecast the evening before and again at breakfast, and start early on both days so the exposed ridge is behind you before the clouds build.

## Food and water
There is no water on the ridge itself, so fill up at the hut and carry two litres per person. Pack lunch for both days, plus nuts, dried fruit and chocolate for the steep sections. Eat a little every hour rather than waiting until you are hungry, and drink before you feel thirsty.

## Fitness preparation
Do a few hill walks with a loaded pack in the weeks before the trip, ideally with 800 metres of climbing, and practise walking downhill with poles. Good boots that are already broken in matter more than any other piece of equipment.

## Getting there
Take the train to the valley station and the local bus to the car park, which runs twice a day in summer; check the timetable because the last bus back leaves early in the evening. Parking at the trailhead is free but limited, so arrive before seven if driving.

## Safety and reservations
The hut is Rifugio Rosa; reserve by phone and confirm the booking a week before. Turn around by 13:00 at the latest if the summit has not been reached, whatever the conditions look like, because afternoon storms build on the exposed ridge. The emergency number is 112; give the location, the number of people and the nature of the problem.
